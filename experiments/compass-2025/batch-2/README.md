# J0722 was already brighter 37–40 hours before its reported outburst discovery

On January 19, 2025, two ZTF exposures caught the helium-accreting binary TCP J07222683+6220548 (J0722, also ASASSN-25aj) substantially above its usual brightness. The New Milky Way survey discovered the bright outburst on January 20.9416 UTC. Tarasenkov and colleagues subsequently identified the system spectroscopically. They found no obvious pre-outburst brightening, a question that matters for how quickly an accreting white dwarf's disk can enter outburst. The January 19 exposures already appear in their Figure 1. We recovered the total brightness in those exposures and compared it with the star's own baseline. ## What the January exposures measure

| Measurement | Green (g) | Red (r) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Hours before the NMW discovery exposure | 40.3 | 36.9 |
| Difference magnitude in the alert and published figure | 20.113 | 19.903 |
| Total magnitude after adding the reference flux | 19.318 ± 0.090 | 19.225 ± 0.090 |
| Total magnitude on the direct image | 19.280 ± 0.067 | 19.218 ± 0.067 |
| Field-corrected brightening above the Jan 2024–Jan 18, 2025 median | 0.566 mag (35 baseline dates) | 0.922 mag (38 baseline dates) |
| Brightening from Jan 17 to Jan 19 using forced photometry | 0.663 mag | 0.621 mag |

An alert magnitude describes the amount of light added to the reference image. In January the star already contributed about magnitude 20 to that reference. Adding the two fluxes reproduces the broker's corrected magnitudes and agrees with the independent direct-image reduction of the same exposures to 0.04 mag. The forced reduction also measures January 17, when the difference images yielded only upper limits. Its quoted one-epoch errors were 0.164 and 0.064 mag in green and 0.105 and 0.054 mag in red on the two respective dates. These reductions share ZTF photons. The filters observed 3.36 hours apart, so a precise instantaneous color is unavailable. [Matched exposure IDs, fluxes and errors](results/broker.json) · [ZTF alert schema](https://zwickytransientfacility.github.io/ztf-avro-alert/schema.html) · [ALeRCE forced photometry](https://tap.alerce.online/tableinfo/ztf.forced_photometry)

Five comparison stars were measured on each January 19 exposure. Against the previous 30 days alone, the target still gained 0.48 mag in green and 0.64 mag in red. No earlier date among **267** dates with both usable, field-corrected filters was as bright in both at once. This is a retrospective comparison after the event drew attention. Earlier faint visits can drop out of the direct source catalog. Both January residuals fall within 0.28″ of the propagated Gaia position and the next cataloged Gaia star is more than 33″ away. [Field controls, earlier dates and selection](results/onset.json) · [Protocol and amendment](onset-protocol.md) · [Images and Gaia positions](images/README.md)

**The evidence supports elevated light before the discovery exposure.** A continuous early rise and a short, separate bright episode would both fit the gap. In the discovery paper, four ATLAS orange points on January 18 and four on January 20 bracket the night, with a 3.29 mag rise between the two medians. The January 19 ASAS-SN limits reach about magnitude 17.2, considerably brighter than the ZTF detections. Figure 1 already records the ZTF observations at their difference magnitudes. The contribution here is the total-light measurement, its field comparison and the resulting constraint on the onset. Scientific priority for that interpretation remains open. ## An April brightening beyond the discovery paper's light curve

The discovery paper describes short rebrightenings near days 30, 39 and 55. Its plotted observations end near day 57. On **April 16, day 85**, direct ZTF measurements reached **g = 16.423 ± 0.015** and **r = 16.922 ± 0.015**, 1.89 hours apart. Relative to the April 5–11 median, the raw gains were 1.15 mag in green and 0.38 mag in red. Relative to April 21–May 3, the gains were 2.03 and 1.36 mag. The event sits on an already elevated, declining light curve. The earlier measurements do not establish a return to quiescence before April 16. [Per-band dates and gaps](results/onset.json) · [April science, reference and difference images](images/README.md)

The original faint comparison stars supported the green change but only two had usable measurements on the April red exposure, below the required three. A separate panel matched to the target's April brightness provided three green and four red controls. With that correction the gains over April 5–11 were 1.15 and 0.38 mag. The largest individual control shift was 0.054 mag in absolute value. Hiroshi Itoh independently observed the episode on April 15. VSOLJ lists 180 exposures from that observer on that single night. The AAVSO search under three names returned no April measurements. The ZTF observations add two calibrated bands to an episode with prior observer coverage. Its peak, duration and publication priority remain uncertain. [Comparison-star records](results/onset.json) · [Observer credit, inventory and contemporary circular](catalog/README.md)

## Two other candidates with known clocks

* **V1153 Aql.** Its bright detections recurred as a Mira variable pulsated. A 321.78-day fit to 322 early red nights predicted 161 later red and 148 later green nights. Without refitting phase, it also ranked the earlier Gaia brightnesses correctly across 2014–17 (Spearman 0.91 in BP and 0.96 in RP). The published 325-day period also worked. ATLAS catalog estimates of 0.994 and 443.38 days both failed the ZTF and Gaia comparisons. The tested doubled-period model can recover the same cycle through its second harmonic. A unique new period or secular change remains unresolved. [Gaia observations, catalog alternatives and phase plot](recurrent/README.md)
* **LINEAR 15938573.** Our pre-2023 red data recovered the cataloged 0.62232-day RR Lyrae pulsation. The fitted 0.622314-day waveform reduced later squared error by 96.6% in red (245 dates) and 96.8% in green (254 dates) relative to the training mean in each filter. Neither an extra radial mode nor a nearby modulation frequency cleared the registered 10% additional improvement in both bands. The same procedure recovered a planted 0.12-mag secondary sinusoid and rejected a stable simulated pulsator. We tested two specific frequency ranges and a simple additive model. ## How this batch was selected and checked

The frozen 2025 search contains 2,110 in-window alerts, 974 distinct ZTF IDs, 132 sampled positions, 131 verified histories and 74 positions eligible for at least one photometric route. Its first round left 65 eligible positions after duplicates and prior cases. This second round admitted the next-ranked source in each route, all at original rank 2. All three have prior identities. **62 eligible positions remain** in the same sample. The three cases each received independent first readings. The onset photometry and two follow-up dates were inspected before the matched field checks, so the follow-ups are exploratory.
