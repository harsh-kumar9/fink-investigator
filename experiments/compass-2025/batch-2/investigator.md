# Investigator's first reading

These assessments use the three cases in the frozen batch packet, before external queries or comparison with the other review.

* **ZTF25aacfjde.** The history has positive detections in both filters on four distinct dates over 12 days. A short brightening of the star flagged as a white-dwarf candidate is plausible. A different transient along the line of sight could produce the same alert history. Compare positions with epoch-corrected catalog sources, check the science and reference images and search for dated reports. Direct measurements or limits before and after January would distinguish an isolated episode from persistent faint variability. The broker's empty TNS field leaves prior reports open.
* **ZTF18aagstwh.** The red and green histories have both signs across hundreds of dates. The RR Lyrae label gives a testable hypothesis of periodic pulsation. A catalog period should predict later direct photometry in both filters. Blending or a position shift could also modulate the residuals. Check the true-light amplitude, centroid by phase, nearby sources and any reported modulation before claiming a new period or a secular change.
* **ZTF18abmfrjj.** Seven years contain pairs of positive green dates. At the anchor the green excess alone is magnitude 13.55. Distinct outbursts are plausible, but a pulsating star observed around its maxima would also pass. Check for returns to a measured faint level, test a periodic alternative and use both bands. Look up a position and aliases in catalogs because the broker returned a service failure.
