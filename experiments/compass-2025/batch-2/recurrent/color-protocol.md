# Is the same brightness a different color on the way up?

The registered timing test recovered the known Mira cycle, and the overall green range exceeded the red range. That prompted this secondary test before inspecting the paired colors, their phase dependence or field corrections. Mira variables can follow loops between photometric colors as their atmospheres change during pulsation. A two-band observation at the same red magnitude on either side of maximum can test this in V1153 Aql. [Alvarez & Plez's multiband observations and atmosphere models](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9710157)

Use the direct-source nightly series through the Fink anchor. Pair a green and a red nightly measurement only if their median exposure times are within 0.25 days. Include red magnitudes 13–15.5 and green magnitudes brighter than 19 to reduce saturation and faint-end incompleteness. Use the blind, red-training period and coefficients fixed in the first test; identify the predicted brightening and fading branches from the sign of the derivative of the fitted red waveform. Require an absolute slope of at least 0.01 mag per day. Across candidate brightening/fading night pairs within a single fitted pulsation cycle centered on maximum, greedily take the smallest absolute red magnitude difference down to a maximum tolerated mismatch of 0.1 mag, using each night once. Break ties by date. The statistic is median (g−r) on fading minus (g−r) on brightening across the matched pairs. Report by cycle and by whether both observations preceded or followed the training cutoff. Keep straddling pairs separate. Treat repeated dates in a cycle as correlated.

For a recurring color difference require at least three pulsation cycles with two or more pairs each, consistent direction across at least three of those cycles, and an absolute median cycle difference of at least 0.15 mag. Require at least one later cycle with two matched pairs whose sign agrees with the earlier cycles before describing the behavior as predictive. Compare results for a tighter red tolerance of 0.05 mag, a catalog period of 325 days, half the date pairing tolerance, and the same-exposure field-corrected target when the field-control rule can be met. Record whether green at the same red brightness was missing more often on one branch. Conditioning on green detections limits conclusions near faint phases. The two filters do not identify a specific molecule, shock or companion.

**Comparison-star amendment.** The original control rule admitted zero stars matching the target in both bands. The 17 raw target pairs gave opposite median directions across the three training cycles and two later cycles. Preserve that result. As a supplementary calibration diagnostic, use separate comparison ensembles per band. Keep the same distance, detector, 40 observations and 1 mag conditions in each band, and retain the three nearest eligible stars per band. The stars are selected before looking at their light curves. Correct individual target exposures using at least two comparisons and their median training-level offsets, then repeat both the frozen clock score and color test. Different comparison stars cannot exclude source-specific color-related systematics.
