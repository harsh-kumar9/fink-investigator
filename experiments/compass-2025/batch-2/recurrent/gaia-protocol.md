**Interpretation of the delivered flags.** The AIP array table marks 45/47 transits with a G-band excess-CCD-scatter indicator. A strict G screen that rejects this indicator and any rejected astrometric CCD leaves no G bins. The indicator describes G only; BP and RP have their own explicit photometry- and variability-rejection flags. Retain the strict G outcome and show a second G diagnostic based on the already averaged transit G photometry, requiring at least five contributing CCDs, accepted variability processing and flux/error above five. Reject photometry- or variability-flagged points and require flux-over-error greater than 5. Use the median magnitude and median time of all accepted samples within each successive 10-day bin anchored to BJD 2455197.5. Rank correlation with the predicted red magnitude allows the overall band offset and amplitude scale to differ. Require at least ten occupied bins and correlation at least 0.5 for recovery on this independent telescope. Repeat for BP and RP, and quote G correlation after leaving out each occupied bin separately. Compare all prespecified aliases and both published periods. Gaia time is barycentric TCB, ZTF time heliocentric. Their difference is negligible relative to a hundreds-of-days Mira cycle; interpretation of daily aliases is less exact. Differences in waveform across filters, changes in pulsation and the limited time span make an old-to-new comparison alone insufficient for measuring secular period change.
