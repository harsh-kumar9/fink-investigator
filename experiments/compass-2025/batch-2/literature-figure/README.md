# The January 19 observations already appear in Figure 1

Tarasenkov et al. discovered and classified TCP J07222683+6220548. Their 2025 paper uses ZTF outburst photometry from Lasair and earlier direct measurements from DR23. Their middle panel includes a green point 1.68 days before the January 20.9416 discovery and their full-history panel includes the corresponding red observation. We located both marker centers in their public gnuplot sources and verified the corresponding colored points in the final journal PDF, page 4. The figure already includes the January 19 detections. [Tarasenkov et al., Figure 1 and Sections 2.4 and 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1088/1674-4527/adddec) · [Authors' arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/src/2505.20842v1)

The last digits above identify the marker comparison. Photometric errors come from the ZTF reductions. On this faint night, the plotted magnitudes agree with the difference-image measurements in both bands. They are 0.83 and 0.68 mag fainter than the respective total-source measurements. Matching the other markers reinforces the identification. Twelve of 13 green outburst markers and 14 of 15 red markers matched a unique same-filter alert in our archive within one plotting unit in time and 0.005 mag in brightness. The middle panel also has six ASAS-SN upper limits on January 19 at 17.13–17.27 mag. All are too shallow to test a source near 19.2 mag. The direct-light comparison to measured quiescence gives physical context that is difficult to read from the published figure. A single elevated night leaves open both ordinary fluctuations and the earliest phase of the rise. [Reproducible comparison](check.py) · [Target images](../images/README.md)

Independent ATLAS orange-band forced photometry brackets the January 19 ZTF exposures. On January 18 (days −2.56 to −2.55), the four orange markers span 19.30–19.89 mag, with a median of 19.43. On January 20 (days −0.41 to −0.36), four more span 15.95–16.41 mag, with a median of 16.14. The 3.29 mag difference between these two orange medians shows a substantial rise across the interval. No orange marker coincides with the January 19 ZTF exposures. The large scatter on January 18, differing passbands and lack of plotted errors preclude a precise estimate of the onset or a direct validation of the January 19 ZTF amplitude. The nearest cyan observations before and after fall around day −40 (four points, 19.57–20.00 mag) and day +3.5 (four points, 12.96–12.98 mag). The authors already describe the rapid rise measured with ATLAS and NMW. [Bracket measurements and panel resolution](results.json) · [Authors' rise analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20842v1#S3.SS1)

The latest observation plotted across the middle panel is at day +57.46, March 19, 2025. The latest marker in the full-history panel is consistent with this at its coarser resolution. Both April 15 and April 16 fall outside the plotted observations. Separate observer reports can establish priority for the April episode.
