# Is there more than one clock in the fading source?

The frozen packet for ZTF18aagstwh reports an RR Lyrae association. Before inspecting the direct magnitudes or catalog periods, register a test for a second pulsation mode or a nearby frequency that could indicate modulation. A previously cataloged pulsation is a control for recovery. A secondary frequency requires its own validation.

Use the same DR24 source per band selected by the compass. Keep unflagged finite measurements with errors in (0, 0.2] mag. Keep the lowest-error exposure per UTC date per filter, breaking ties by exposure ID. Use heliocentric observation times for phase. Learn all frequencies using red dates before 2023. Reserve 2023 onward in each band. Fit each band's own coefficients on its pre-2023 dates. Require at least 80 training dates and 40 later dates per band.

1. Search 1 to 5 cycles per day for the dominant training frequency with a floating-mean Lomb–Scargle periodogram. Refine the best peak by minimizing the training squared error of a five-harmonic Fourier fit within one frequency resolution element. Keep this as the single-clock baseline.
2. Search its red training residuals in two prespecified families. For a second radial mode consider periods 0.70–0.80 times the dominant period and the reciprocal range, allowing either radial mode to dominate. For a nearby modulation frequency search both sides of the fundamental from 1/400 to 1/10 cycles per day away. For each family choose one peak by training power, then refine by minimizing the joint training squared error with an added sinusoid. Record the daily-alias distances of the resulting frequencies. These searches can also detect harmonics of an incorrect fundamental, sampling aliases or a changing waveform.
3. Carry each pair of frequencies into both later bands. For each band compare the five-harmonic single clock and the same model with the added sinusoid. Require a reduction of at least 10% in held-out squared errors against the single clock in **both** bands before calling the extra frequency a lead. Report the underlying single-clock performance against the training mean. Make the same measurements on a synthetic stable pulsator and on one with a secondary sinusoid of semi-amplitude 0.12 mag at a period ratio of 0.745, each with the actual sampling and deterministic independent Gaussian noise scaled by the quoted errors. These injections test recovery under simple noise, not a false-positive rate for actual stars.
