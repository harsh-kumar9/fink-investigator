# Follow-up on the two dates at J0722

Inspection of the 50 archived detections and the primary DR24 series highlighted January 19 before the reported discovery and April 16 during the decline. Both dates and filters have already been seen. The following comparisons were set before retrieving any comparison-star measurements, secondary-field photometry or independent April detections. An initial April AAVSO query by TCP name returned a header with no rows.

* Measure the January 19 target against the median of all available earlier 2024 January 1 through 2025 January 18 dates, and against the previous 30 days separately. For the empirical rarity in ordinary conditions, compare January 19 with **all** pre-January 19 paired g/r dates, using the same one-year fixed zero points. Count earlier dates at least as bright in both bands; correlations, selection of the date and missing faint measurements preclude a formal false-alarm probability.
* Compare April 16 with April 5–11 and April 21–May 3 separately by band. Compute elapsed times from NMW's JD 2460696.4416. Display the available measurements and preceding/following observational gaps. Require both filters to exceed their respective baseline by 0.3 mag in each comparison to describe a sampled late brightening. This is a retrospective check. A separate outburst with a return to quiescence is a stronger requirement.
* Query IRSA sources in the same field, CCD and quadrant as the selected target within 120″. Per filter use the five sources closest in archival mean magnitude among those 10–120″ away with at least 100 catalog observations, mean within 1.5 mag of the target and at least 5″ from one another. Select without reading their light curves. For each comparison star subtract its median before January 19 in the same 2024–25 window. Correct each target exposure by the median residual of at least three available stars on the exact exposure, then reduce to nightly medians. Retain raw and corrected comparisons, individual control shifts and actual numbers. For April also give target time separation between filters. A color change over non-simultaneous exposures is ambiguous.
* Check all other DR24 series in the original 1.5″ source cone on both event dates, retaining zero counts. Check the AAVSO International Database and primary reports for contemporaneous observations. Report an independent telescope measurement only if its date, source, band and observer can be verified. A missing observation is a gap. Search the direct series after April for dates within 0.3 mag of the pre-event median and any subsequent two-date return to at least 2 mag above baseline, with dates separated by at most 14 days. Duration and new-outburst claims require sampling appropriate to the claim.

The original red comparison set had only two usable stars on April 16. Preserve this missing result. As a sensitivity check choose a second panel before downloading its time series, using the original cone and isolation rule and matching its mean magnitude to the observed **bright-state** value (g 16.4, r 16.9) within 1.5 mag. Keep the same five-star quota, three-star exposure minimum and baseline. This tests changes shared by stars of comparable brightness on the event night. Also compare ALeRCE's reference-corrected detections and forced measurements with the January 17 and 19 direct images. All three reductions of a single exposure share a telescope and photons.
