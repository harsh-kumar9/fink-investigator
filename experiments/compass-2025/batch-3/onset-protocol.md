# Was the April–May transient bright twice?

The selected Fink history at ZTF25aaohzdc contains five good positive alerts on three dates. Green was bright on April 24 and May 16, 2025, with a much fainter green alert on May 1. This motivated an exploratory test before reading independent light curves or the archive of science exposures.

* Retrieve public ZTF forced photometry and dated limits from the ALeRCE broker. Compare the five alert magnitudes with the same exposure and band, separating difference flux and total-source flux. Search all returned forced dates for additional bright states and constraining limits, keeping processing flags and uncertainty. A detection on another reduction of the same exposure checks the reduction. Independent observation requires another exposure or telescope.
* Retrieve the public IRSA science image inventory at the alert coordinates in April–June 2025. For every available g/r image from April 1 through June 15 with science type, infobits below 33554432, seeing at most 3.5″ and image magnitude limit at least 19, measure light on the original image at the same sky position. Choose the deepest exposure per filter per UTC night, breaking ties by earlier time, without using target flux. Retain any missing cutouts. Measure a local background and fixed aperture, use same-frame comparison stars for calibration, and estimate noise with offset apertures. Show faint or negative flux estimates as well as significant detections.
* Require two dates at least 1.5 mag brighter in a **single band** than an intervening measured date or sufficiently deep upper limit. Report dated brackets for changes and the extent of quiescent coverage. A sampling gap during a dip leaves the duration and physical nature of the two bright states undetermined. Compare archival counterparts and earlier time coverage before suggesting a variable-star or transient classification. Record archival identifiers and literature priority separately.

## A search for a separate eruption

The ASAS-SN discovery record identifies a March outburst at this position. The image inventory, inspected before reducing any cutout, has no March 2025 ZTF visit. Test for another bright episode in the next available observing season using the deepest qualifying image on every observed UTC date in 2026. Use the same image-quality criteria as above, allowing either g or r. As a historical comparison, take the qualifying date nearest the 15th of each observed calendar month in 2018–24, with earlier dates preferred on ties, and choose the deepest visit on that date. This sparse comparison has little sensitivity to short eruptions between visits. Save the survey dates and the magnitudes they can test. Review any detection brighter than 18 mag with signal-to-noise of at least five in the target aperture. Use a second exposure or telescope and image inspection before promoting a new eruption.

Use a circular four-pixel radius on original science cutouts, a local annulus of 7–11 pixels, and the median of at least three comparison-star zero points from the same detector and filter. Choose sources of 15–18.5 mean magnitude with at least 30 catalog observations, 14–65″ from the target and no comparably bright catalog source within 8″. Use offset apertures within the cutout, separated from cataloged sources, to estimate background scatter. Include a comparison-star dispersion term for quoted errors. Save actual zero points, sky scatter and control residuals. If a detector has insufficient calibrators, retain the row as unmeasured. A qualifying independent measurement brighter than 18 mag outside the 2025 window would motivate checking the surrounding observing dates.

Before reading the historical scan, reserve the standard closest to 18 mag (object ID breaks ties) for a leave-one-out check in each image with at least four usable standards. Fit the other stars and measure whether that control is detected at five sigma and lies within 0.3 mag of its catalog mean. From exposures reaching magnitude 18, calculate the fraction of possible continuous start times for a 1-, 3-, 7- or 14-day plateau that would intersect at least one measurement. Report both the interval from the first to last tested night in 2026 and the longer calendar window from January 1 to November 26. The model assumes the source stays brighter than 18 in either observed filter for the entire specified duration. The result measures cadence conditional on brightness and duration.
