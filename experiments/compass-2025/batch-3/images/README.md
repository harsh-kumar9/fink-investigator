# Imaging and archival light from the three positions

**ASASSN-25ba (ZTF25aaohzdc).** On April 24 and May 16, 2025, a bright compact peak in the green science image coincides with the difference residual. The peak is much fainter on May 1. No clear object is visible at that position in the displayed ZTF templates. Two stars are visible to one side, and the nearest Gaia DR3 star was 10.53″ from the alert after proper-motion correction. Earlier Pan-STARRS images resolve a faint object at the transient position. [Green image triplets](ZTF25aaohzdc/montage.png) · [Gaia data](ZTF25aaohzdc/gaia.json) · [Pan-STARRS check](ps1/README.md)

**ZTF18aazutgw.** Each of the three red difference images shows a compact central negative residual, with separate subtraction structure elsewhere. This is a crowded field. Gaia DR3 lists 392 sources within 60″. The central residuals lie close to Gaia DR3 4295582339667975168 (G = 14.28). The median separation from this moving position was 0.136″ over 135 positive dates and 0.067″ over 313 negative dates. The nearest other star is G = 18.74 at 2.61″. That star could contribute to total light during a deep minimum, while the residual centroids favor the brighter star as the varying source. [Three red exposures](ZTF18aazutgw/montage.png) · [Measurements](results.json)

**ZTF17aabshbq.** The green difference images show a compact positive residual in October 2022 and negative residuals in November 2019 and December 2025. The same central star is visible in the reference and science images at all three dates. Gaia DR3 964727238971174400 is 0.086″ from the anchor. Median radial offsets to the propagated star were 0.091″ on 211 positive dates and 0.090″ on 161 negative dates. The next Gaia star lies 15.37″ away. A light curve can distinguish a sequence of underlying brightness changes from repeated crossings of the reference flux. [Three green exposures](ZTF17aabshbq/montage.png) · [Gaia data](ZTF17aabshbq/gaia.json)
