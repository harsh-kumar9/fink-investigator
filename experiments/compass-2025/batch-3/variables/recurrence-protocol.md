# Is there a characteristic outburst spacing?

Five of six well-sampled ZTF intervals fell between 14 and 20 days. The broad timing search found no single early-red waveform that predicted later years. Before examining the ordering of the full ASAS-SN flux series outside the 68-day follow-up, register a test of recurrence without assuming a stable phase. The measured ZTF intervals, 2018–25 red curves and ASAS-SN comparison within the 68-day interval motivate this analysis. Prior analysis used the complete set of common dates only to calculate one cross-survey rank correlation and typical levels in bright/faint states.

Use all quality-accepted ASAS-SN nightly forced fluxes from 2019 through the Fink anchor. Exclude November 10, 2020 through January 17, 2021 and the six red-selected onset-to-onset intervals, inclusive, from both ends of every tested pair. Within each UTC calendar year, consider a night and all later available nights 6–10 days, 14–20 days, and 24–29 days away. Take each range's median absolute flux difference from that night. Retain only nights with at least one comparison in all three ranges. For each year measure the three medians across these common anchor nights. If eruptions often recur after about 17 days, the 14–20-day group should be more similar than both comparison groups. Require at least ten matched anchors per year and at least three qualifying years in which the middle-lag difference is smaller than both controls. Report failures and individual years. The overlapping pairs and changing sampling preclude a simple independent-pairs significance test. A null result can coexist with isolated two-to-three-week intervals.

**Checks after seeing the five positive years.** The 14–20-day interval includes roughly half a lunar month. For each evaluated calendar year, fit camera intercepts and two harmonics at a fixed 29.53059-day frequency on accepted measurements from all other years, then subtract that prediction and repeat the three-lag comparison on the original test-year calendar. Independently check nearby Sky Patrol sources observed on the identical images. Search the master catalog inside 60″ of MGAB-V243. Select at most three other sources at least 30″ away, nearest first, with Gaia counterparts within 2″ and Gaia G within 1.5 mag of the target. Choose controls before reading their light curves. On common images with measured fluxes, subtract each comparator's median from its flux, subtract the median comparator deviation from the target with at least two controls on an exposure, then repeat the same test on corrected fluxes. Report the achieved coverage; faint comparison targets can be absent from Sky Patrol's input catalog. If coverage is insufficient, use independently calibrated ZTF green flux dates after the clock-training cutoff and outside the discovery intervals as a separate source-level check. Retain these as sensitivity checks on the measured recurrence rather than new independent primary tests.
