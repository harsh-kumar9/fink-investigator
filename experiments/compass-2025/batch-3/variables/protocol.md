# A clock or an evolving state?

Fixed after the previous batch and the new source positions were known, before examining the direct light curves, external catalog labels or periods of either new variable. Evaluate both ZTF17aabshbq and ZTF18aazutgw identically. The calendar window for this comparison stops at each source's anomaly anchor. For an anchor later than the DR24 release ends, use the actually available span.

* Use the same direct-source and quality criteria as the main compass, nightly median magnitudes and median HJD for each date. Search the earliest two thirds of the red nights with a two-harmonic Lomb–Scargle model from 0.1 to 1,000 days at ten frequencies per training-baseline resolution element. Refit the top frequency locally by exact least squares. At the resulting period, half-period, double-period, and sidereal-day and annual aliases, freeze the early red fit and predict later red magnitudes. Transfer the red waveform to green by fitting a constant and a scale on green training dates before the same cutoff. Record red and green error reductions relative to their training mean, rank correlation, and the same score on green dates without red measurements. Require at least 30 test dates per band, at least 20% error reduction and a rank correlation of 0.5 in each band to call the clock recovered. Earlier catalog periods identified independently can be tested on exactly the same dates. A doubled-period harmonic often fits the same waveform.
* Measure broad changes in brightness that a timing search could miss. In each band separately use 30-day bins anchored at MJD 58000 with at least three distinct dates. Find the most pronounced bright-to-faint or faint-to-bright change on red bins (absolute median contrast), comparing the preceding and following 180 days, requiring three bins on each side. Resolve ties by earlier boundary. Describe this interval as selected in red. At that fixed boundary compare green bin medians using the same date windows and cuts, record the number and date span of bins, repeat with each bin omitted, and repeat restricting green to dates without a red measurement. Require same-direction absolute changes ≥0.5 mag in both filters and ≥0.3 mag after dropping any one bin. A large amplitude repetitive star can pass just by changing phase. If a recovered clock exists, repeat the state test on its residuals and interpret a raw change as a possible state change only if the residual change also passes. Seasonal gaps limit any inferred transition date.
* Compare the strongest inference for each source with actual published classifications, periods or epoch photometry. For a clock, an earlier telescope can be tested by extrapolating the frozen red phases and ranking measurements without a phase fit. For a putative change, obtain another survey's measurements in the same windows when available. Record controls, nearby-source or instrumental alternatives, acquisition errors and ambiguous outcomes.
