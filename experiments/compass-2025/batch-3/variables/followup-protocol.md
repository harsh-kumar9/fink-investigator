# Follow-up on a dwarf nova and a Mira

The initial blind search returned a 379.74-day clock for the Mira IRAS 19245+0727. A red-only half-year change in dwarf nova MGAB-V243 had insufficient green coverage. The following tests were fixed after these first results and before downloading independent epoch photometry.

**IRAS 19245+0727.** Score the independently identified 385.1017206-day VSX period on the same ZTF dates. Compare the blind, published, double-, half-, annual and sidereal-day alias fits against earlier Gaia DR3 BP and RP observations of source 4295582339667975168. Keep photometry- and variability-accepted positive-S/N (>5) points in each band. Bin successive ten-day visits, anchored at BJD 2455197.5, and compare their median magnitudes against the unchanged early red prediction by rank correlation (at least ten bins and correlation ≥0.5). Use G transit-average photometry as a secondary comparison with at least five contributing CCDs, variability acceptance and the same S/N threshold. Compare also to a pure 365.25-day fit trained in red. Gaia and ZTF use different filters. The data can discriminate a physical cycle from annual observing cadence without determining an exact fundamental or period change.

**MGAB-V243.** Count resolved returns to bright state in red, with the early training 25th percentile in magnitude defining bright and the 75th defining faint. Group bright nights until an observed faint night separates them. For an episode require two bright red nights within ten days and report whether a green measurement was at least as bright as its own training 25th percentile within two nights of either. Estimate the interval between adjacent first observed bright dates only if both groups are resolved, an intervening faint measurement exists, and no red observing gap between the two first dates exceeds five days. Report all intervals, observing coverage and censoring. These first-observed dates can lag true starts.

Query the MAST TESSCut footprint at MGAB-V243. Take the first four sectors in numerical order with sector ≤93 (before the end of ZTF DR24), or all if fewer. Request 9 × 9 pixels, preserve coverage and raw FITS hashes, and reduce each six-hour bin containing at least five quality-zero, finite exposures to the median pixel image. On each exposure subtract the median outer border level. Compare the sum of the central 3 × 3 pixels against each ZTF band's direct nightly magnitudes (nearest six-hour bin within 0.25 days). Compare four 3 × 3 corner apertures under identical selection. Difference the median pixel images at the brightest and faintest thirds of ZTF red nights; report the positive peak pixel's distance to the WCS target. Require six red matched nights, central flux-vs-brightness rank correlation ≥0.65 and a difference peak within 1.5 TESS pixels to associate a sector's signal. Green and the corner apertures can expose conflicting interpretations. A TESS pixel spans about 21″. Compare provenance of any prior reports to separate independent telescopes from republished ZTF alerts. [MAST TESSCut service](https://mast.stsci.edu/tesscut/)

**Mira survey cross-check.** The stringent Gaia cuts left seven BP and two RP bins; the secondary Gaia G diagnostic favored the blind period over 365.25 days. Before downloading Sky Patrol measurements of the Mira, test the same predictions on ASAS-SN forced fluxes. Use the nearest master-catalog source within 3″, report any other source inside 6″, and select finite quality-G fluxes with positive finite errors, including negative flux. Retain dates from 2019 onward for the g-band comparison. Bin by ten days anchored at MJD 58000. Score the fixed red waveform by rank on bins lying completely after its training cutoff and separately on earlier bins, requiring at least ten bins and a correlation of 0.5. Compare the published, annual and blind frequencies without changing phase. ASAS-SN's wider aperture, broad optical band and seasonal window affect the interpretation. This comparison supplements the prespecified Gaia test.

After independent catalog review found the exact single-band alternatives in Chen et al. (194.0059046 and 188.3239431 days) and Gaia DR3 frequency (period 380.6680363 days), repeat the same fixed training and test date selections at all three periods. These are descriptive comparisons on already scored dates. The published Gaia period and the downloaded Gaia epochs derive from the same mission data.
