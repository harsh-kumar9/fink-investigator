# First wide run of the discovery compass

Registered before retrieving the 2025 anomaly list. Use the complete UTC calendar year 2025, in twelve disjoint monthly windows. Measure calendar coverage and the number of unique ZTF IDs in the entire returned archive. It contains Fink's nightly selections. Review capacity is 12 histories per month. Keep the lowest anomaly-score packet per ID per month (earlier time then smaller candid on ties). Assign an ID to its first month of appearance in the year. Partition each month's new IDs by (1) nonnegative age ≤90 days from the packet's reported first detection, (2) other positive residuals, and (3) everything else. From each stratum select up to four anchors evenly spaced in time, including the ends. Fill unused slots by the same rule from all remaining anchors. Use time, ID and candid for deterministic ordering. Save every archive response, request, checksum, selected ID and its anchor. Overlaps with earlier project cases stay visible in the sample but cannot take a new review slot.

Keep source labels out of route scoring. The three questions below use ZTF g and r detections up to the selected packet, DRB ≥0.8, and 0 < magnitude error ≤0.2. Count distinct UTC dates; use data from a single filter for brightness comparisons. Require the selected packet itself to meet the same quality and filter requirements.

* **Recent onset.** The anchor is positive and its reported first-detection age is 0–90 days. The observed quality history through the anchor also spans ≤90 days. Require at least three positive dates and positive detections in both filters. Rank by the number of dates with both filters positive, then by positive date count.
* **Repeated bright detections.** In a single filter, find two pairs of positive dates. Each pair spans 1–30 days and the pairs are separated by at least 90 days from the end of the first to the start of the second. Rank by number of distinct UTC years containing such a pair, then by that filter's positive-date count. Gaps in the alert stream leave quiescence unmeasured.
* **Repeated fading.** In a single filter require at least five negative dates spanning ≥90 days and five positive dates. Rank by number of distinct UTC years containing negative detections, then by the smaller of the two sign counts in that filter. The sign is relative to the reference. Direct photometry is necessary to measure the depth.

Use the alphabetically first object ID to break ranking ties. Select at most one case per question in the order above. Skip previously admitted IDs, positions within 5″ of an earlier selected case or a previously studied project position or control, and identified physical aliases during adjudication. Keep these exclusions and all unreviewed eligible objects in the ledger. Two route matches for a single object still represent one source. A cataloged variable or explosion can have a measurable, previously untested behavior. Archive the catalog history as context.

Replay the original seven-source gate. Also challenge these routing rules on real labeled sources outside the 2025 sample with expectations recorded separately before scoring. Save control failures; interpret each route in light of its own controls. For the three case slots, request independent investigator and skeptic first readings of the same evidence. Compare their predictions to dated source measurements, registry records or direct-image photometry. Record alternatives, outcomes and the observation that could reverse each interpretation. These operational thresholds and purposive samples do not estimate survey-wide rates.
