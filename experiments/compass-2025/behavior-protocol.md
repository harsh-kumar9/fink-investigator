# Follow-up after identifying FT Cam and V1060 Cyg

The first two-group comparison found that only FT Cam's late pair was bright relative to its usual direct magnitude. The other long-lived source is the known dwarf nova V1060 Cyg. Two follow-ups were fixed after seeing the route-level medians and the prior identities, before scanning the full direct nightly curves for episodes or fitting their time dependence.

**FT Cam.** Use the previously selected direct series through the August 24 anchor. Define each filter's typical level as the median across all its quality-selected UTC dates. Mark red dates at least 2 magnitudes brighter than typical. Group marked dates into a possible episode until a direct red measurement returns to within 0.5 mag of typical or fainter. Count a resolved bright episode only if it contains at least two marked red dates within ten days of each other. Check whether green was also at least 2 mag brighter than its own typical level on a date within two days of one of those red dates. Report dates, gaps, distinct red episodes, and the number corroborated in green. Count only directly observed separations between episodes. Sparse cadence can merge or miss events.

**V1060 Cyg.** An earlier Gaia-based study reported a 572 ± 25 day quasi-period in the quiescent level from 59 observations spanning 1,207 days. Test whether a constant-frequency sinusoid at 572 days transfers into modern ZTF observations. In each filter use the faint-side 75th percentile magnitude in fixed 30-day bins with at least three directly measured nights. Use bins anchored to MJD 58000. Fit an offset, sine and cosine before January 2023, and evaluate complete bins from January 2024 through the September 2025 anchor with coefficients fixed. Phases and offsets are fitted separately by filter, so this tests frequency transfer. Compare squared prediction error against the training mean. Report fitted half-amplitude, held-out error reduction and rank agreement for each band. Require eight held-out bins per filter, at least 20% error reduction and rank correlation ≥0.5 in both filters to call the projected variation recovered. Repeat descriptively for 547 and 597 days, for an annual sinusoid, and for the median and 90th percentile at 572 days. The quantiles approximate quiescence and the survey may miss faint states. A negative outcome bears on a persistent clock under this test. The paper described quasi-periodic variability.
