# Does the missing light come from the same position as the extra light?

Freeze this independent extension after selection by the original three routes and before inspecting any target's sign-dependent astrometry. Check every selected ID with a verified 2025 anchor in its history. Restrict rows to that anchor time, both bands handled separately, using the original DRB and magnitude-error cuts, signed residuals and finite ICRS positions. Anchor quality itself does not veto a useful historical series.

Convert each centroid to east and north arcseconds relative to its fixed original anchor, allowing RA wrap and applying cos(dec). For each UTC date, filter and sign take coordinate-wise medians across all usable centroids. Pair positive and negative dates up to 30 days apart, greedily shortest gap first, then earliest positive and earliest negative date to break ties. A mixed-sign date can form one zero-lag pair. No UTC date may be reused by another pair in a given filter. For every pair compute the vector from the negative to the positive centroid.

Order pairs by their later date and then their earlier date. Use the earliest two thirds by pair count, rounding down, for training. Drop later-set pairs with a first date at or before the end of training to ensure both test observations are later. Require at least eight training pairs and four test pairs. Take the component-wise median vector separately in training and test. Require training length ≥0.45″, held-out projection along its direction ≥0.35″, and a test direction within 45° of training. Rank qualified filter results by the smaller of training length and test projection. Break ties by g before r for the same object and then by ZTF ID across objects. Show all counts and measured outcomes, including failures and earlier-source overlaps. Carry the original study's five-arcsecond and ID exclusions forward, and exclude the three cases already admitted this year. Admit at most one new case.

The frozen synthetic controls use 24 non-overlapping sign pairs near an RA wrap at declination 60°. They test a stable offset of (+0.60″,+0.45″), a shared linear drift with alternating sign order and zero intrinsic offset, an offset reversing direction in the last eight pairs, and 11 pairs with stable offset but insufficient coverage. Expected decisions are pass, fail, fail and insufficient, respectively. These controls check geometry, leakage, units and threshold behavior. They do not calibrate a sky false-positive rate. Both passbands can share instrumental errors. Sign-dependent seeing, reference subtraction, differential refraction, proper motion and neighboring variable components are competing explanations for a displacement.
