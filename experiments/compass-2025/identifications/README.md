# Which sources were already known?

The three primary routes recovered a spectroscopically classified supernova and two named dwarf novae. These identities give three different interpretations to positive and negative residuals. An explosion can brighten relative to an older reference. A dwarf nova can repeatedly rise above the reference or return below it after outburst. The identity of the source also sets the bar for claiming a newly measured behavior.

The offsets use the returned catalog coordinates and the Fink anchor in ICRS degrees. The three primary SIMBAD cones have 5″ radii. The astrometric case has a 10″ SIMBAD cone. All variability-catalog cones have 5″ radii. Each offset in the table is a fixed-coordinate separation. Epoch propagation and image centroids are examined separately. **SN 2025oq.** The Caltech Bright Transient Survey returns a single row in the small coordinate box. It gives the exact ZTF alias, discovery observation on January 17, 2025 at 00:35 UTC, peak red magnitude 15.464, redshift 0.01133, and subtype Ic-BL. GOTO's Liverpool Telescope SPRAT spectrum on January 19 gave a tentative Type Ic classification. A new spectrum on February 5 supported the broad-lined subtype. Fink's February 3 packet preserved the earlier label. The explosion was already publicly reported when Fink selected this packet. An empty SIMBAD cone alone would have missed that history. [BTS request and checksum](bts.csv.json) · [TNS discovery and classification reports 20200 and 20311](https://www.wis-tns.org/index.php/object/2025oq) · [Early observation chronology](https://www.wis-tns.org/node/201177)

**FT Cam.** SIMBAD and VSX both identify this position as FT Cam. The ASAS-SN 2021 catalog lists a dwarf nova 1.40″ away and a period of 0.13966 days. Thorstensen and Fenton measured the 0.07492(8)-day orbital period from radial velocities near minimum light. Their time-series photometry also showed flickering. This history makes a return to quiescence between outbursts plausible. Neither catalog period measures the time between the selected pairs of positive alerts. The 5″ ATLAS candidate-variable cone returned zero rows. [VSX entry](https://vsx.aavso.org/index.php?view=detail.top&oid=4716) · [Radial velocities and photometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0209172) · [Catalog queries and checksum](ZTF18abodmfy.xml.json)

**V1060 Cyg.** SIMBAD identifies the source as a cataclysmic variable. VSX and ASAS-SN classify it as an SS Cyg-type dwarf nova. VSX lists 0.292 days. The ATLAS pipeline returned a *dubious* classification with widely differing period estimates, including 6.77 and 230.59 days. Published spectroscopy had already detected the secondary star. Repeated fading below a ZTF reference could reflect quiescence between outbursts; a count of negative nights cannot identify eclipses. [VSX entry](https://vsx.aavso.org/index.php?view=detail.top&oid=11980) · [Liu et al. spectroscopy](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999ApJS..122..243L/abstract) · [Catalog queries and checksum](ZTF18aazenpc.xml.json)

**ZTF18adjrqpw.** The one SIMBAD object within 10″ is CRTS J162520.3+120309, 0.105″ from the Fink anchor. VSX identifies SDSS J162520.29+120308.7 just 0.076″ away. The ATLAS and ASAS-SN cones returned zero entries. Olech and colleagues measured a likely orbital period of 0.09111(15) days and a mean superhump period of 0.095942(17) days in the 2010 outburst. The variable's identity is secure. Measuring the red positions of opposite-sign residuals tests how light from this known system appears in subtraction. [2010 spectroscopy and observing campaign](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201116498) · [VSX entry](https://vsx.aavso.org/index.php?view=detail.top&oid=234063) · [SIMBAD receipt](ZTF18adjrqpw.simbad.csv.json)

## Independent observer coverage

In the AAVSO International Database the early FT Cam interval and its 30-day surroundings contained four visual upper limits. The later interval and its surroundings contained two clear, V-calibrated CCD detections by K. Sokolovsky (SKA), 13.35 and 13.34 mag on August 18–19, about six hours apart in two cameras. Neither of the two registered positive-pair intervals contained a qualifying instrumental measurement. For V1060 Cyg, the year through September 1, 2025 contained 38 observer records, 34 of them limits. Only four instrumental detections passed the quality cuts. No single observer and filter covered five nights with each sign. These independent records provide context, while the two primary photometric tests rely on the archived ZTF direct measurements. All received records have the AAVSO prevalidation flag Z. From this directory, run `python3 check.py`, `python3 check.py --astrometry` and `python3 observers.py` to regenerate the three JSON summaries offline. The first two validate all source requests and hashes and independently recompute the angular separations. The last replays the Fink history and AAVSO time selections. The spectroscopic interpretation uses the cited primary observing reports.
