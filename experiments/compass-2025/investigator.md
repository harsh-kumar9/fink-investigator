# Investigator's first reading of the 2025 cases

Recorded from the common [review packet](results/packet.json) before looking up names, direct photometry or the other review.

* **ZTF25aaccmjq.** Six positive dates in 17 days, three of them in both colors, support a real transient. The packet's TNS label favors a previously reported Type Ic supernova. A variable projected onto a galaxy could produce the photometric onset too. Find a dated classification spectrum, check the transient coordinates against the anchor, and compare the discovery date to January 17. Evidence IDs `position`, `anchor`, `history`, `context`.
* **ZTF18abodmfy.** Repeated positive pairs in seven years and both residual signs support variability. The packet favors a cataclysmic variable. It offers two specific intervals in which to check for outbursts. A regularly varying or persistently bright source can produce the same pairs when observing conditions select a particular phase. Look for individually bounded bright episodes and returns to the faint state in same-filter direct images. Evidence IDs `position`, `anchor`, `history`, `context`.
* **ZTF18aazenpc.** This source repeatedly crossed the reference brightness in both bands. The positive anchor and historical negative dates describe different phases. A known accreting variable could do this as it alternates between bright and faint states. A moving or blended star in imperfect subtraction could also produce signed residuals. Check whether direct magnitudes are fainter on negative dates, and whether negative centroids remain on the expected source as its position evolves. Evidence IDs `position`, `anchor`, `history`, `context`.
