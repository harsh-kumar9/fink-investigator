# Does MGAB-V960 repeatedly return to a bright state every twenty days?

The two-source registered duration screen produced 13 singly or multiply sampled bright groups with faint returns bracketing both sides within seven days for MGAB-V960. In the 2019 season, five such groups began Aug 4, Aug 24, Sep 14, Oct 4 and Oct 23. That sequence was noticed after seeing the event dates in all seasons. The within-sequence ZTF spacing is descriptive. We have inspected neither the actual independent 2019 ASAS-SN measurements nor the following lag, folded or period-forecast scores. The all-date ASAS/ZTF green correlation is 0.29 on 152 dates.

* Fit integer cycle number 0–2 to the first three 2019 event start dates. Do not readjust this clock to later events. Give residuals on the final two selected events. Test the corresponding ephemeris in independent forced flux between Aug 1 and Oct 31, 2019, comparing nights within 3 days of peak phase to nights within 3 days of half phase. Use one median flux per UTC date and one vote per bright/half-phase interval. Report coverage and flux in every observed interval, leave-one-cycle results, and calculate the observed flux contrast for each of 100 equal phase shifts of the fixed model. Test *also* against local ASAS-SN comparators measured on the same image. Choose up to three catalog sources 30–60″ from the target with median outside-2019 flux 0.5–2 times the target's, ranking by distance. Remove each comparison's median per camera on the outside-2019 dates, then subtract the median of ≥2 comparator residuals on each identical image. Show all control-source scores. If too few comparators, preserve the restricted coverage and individual camera scores. This is one proposed fixed ephemeris over this interval and one external telescope. Report overlapping TESS data separately if present.
* Beyond 2019, reuse the anchor-matched absolute flux difference statistic from batch three. Before evaluating, set 8–12, 17–23 and 28–32 days as half-, full- and one-and-a-half-cycle windows. Use at least ten same-year anchor dates with comparison dates in all three windows; report eligible and passing years for red and green independently and ASAS-SN, requiring at least three passing years in each telescope/filter for repeated evidence at this *characteristic* timescale. The 2019 season is kept separate. Keep rates for every season, including failures, and run the full test unchanged on the selected ASAS field stars. Full and half-period aliases, gaps, dates shared by the ZTF filters, censoring and period evolution can affect these tests.
* For a phase-predictive within-season test, assess each year with ≥40 independent red dates. In the first chronological half of that year only, search 501 uniformly spaced frequencies from 1/26 to 1/14 cycles/day in one harmonic of red flux. Fit an offset plus sine/cosine. Keep years with a training span ≥60 days and at least ten later dates spanning ≥40 days. Freeze the epoch, period and shape, per filter fitting the early offset and scale; report held-out red and green gains/correlations without phase adjustment. External forced flux gets an offset and scale from its early season and prediction on the later dates. Apply the original 20% gain, 0.5 rank-correlation criterion to later seasons with ≥10 points. This forecast uses one frequency grid per eligible year in addition to the initial all-period scan and date-sequence inspection.
