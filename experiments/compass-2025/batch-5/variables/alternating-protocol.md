# Are the ten-day events alternating in strength?

After checking the phase of the 2019 sequence, median ZTF flux around each of the five bracketed short events was systematically lower than half a cycle away. Inspection of the two ZTF filters in that season showed bright eruptions between successive short events. This second question arose after seeing the 2019 sequence. Exclude 2019 from the cross-season confirmation and distinguish measured sample maxima from physical maxima.

1. Tabulate every primary bright group of MGAB-V960 from July 15 through October 31, 2019, reporting start/end, dates of preceding and following faint measurements, and brightest measured red/green magnitude in the group. Use only actual qualifying bright dates. In the first three short events, compare their observed red maxima to the maximum of each of the two groups that lie near the intervening half-phases. Set a fixed strength boundary at the midpoint of the median short and intervening red peak magnitudes. Repeat in green independently. Evaluate the last two short events and their two intervening groups without changing thresholds. Give actual individual measurements and errors; a sampled peak bounds the true maximum from below in flux.
2. Apply those frozen two thresholds to every other calendar year. A transition is eligible if both adjacent observed groups contain an actual red magnitude, are separated by a measured faint return and their brightest observed red dates are 5–15 days apart. Count transitions across the fixed red strength boundary. Use only pairs where green measurements are present in both groups for the second-band check, computing green labels from their own threshold. Use an intentionally stricter red quality criterion of two red detections on bright group dates for each event as a sampling sensitivity check. Preserve the chronological graph and observed degree of coverage of each group. Within each year and one-red-date vs multiple-red-dates strata, permute whole event labels jointly for red and green 5,000 times to compare how often mixed-strength pairs occur by chance. Retain the actual cadence and missingness. List the number of groups and eligible links behind each percentage. If this pattern fails beyond 2019, limit it to the single season.
3. On TESS use a local background correction prompted by failed raw-image peak localization: fit each of the 81 pixels on principal components 1–4 of the outer (Chebyshev distance from center ≥3) pixel time series, with median-centered and robust-scatter-normalized comparators. Fit per sector on six-hour images at dates outside ±1.5 days of the independently selected ZTF eruption peaks. Preserve actual target signals by leaving central pixels out of the comparison basis. Report uncorrected and corrected 1×1, 3×3 and off-center apertures, the position and amplitude of the brightest corrected difference pixel, and source-to-control rank correlations. For the 2019 phase contrast compare three-day intervals centered on the actual ZTF-sampled peak dates with measured faint return dates. A pixel localization within one pixel and a positive brightness response in multiple sectors are necessary for an independent attribution. A correction can suppress a true signal if source flux enters the control pixels.

**Matched-time control.** After the corrected TESS images localized the ZTF-bright vs ZTF-faint change in all three 2019 sectors, test whether a shifted sequence could produce the same result. Roll the raw six-hour TESS image sequence together within each sector in 100 equal steps before fitting the local comparison components on the original ZTF-excluded training calendar. The observed roll is zero. Preserve ZTF dates and magnitudes, cutout geometry and the TESS flux covariance. Require the same joint source-position and correlation thresholds as the real sequence in both filters in each sector. Report successful rolls by sector and under the same fractional shift across sectors. These correlated controls diagnose alignment; they do not provide a false-alarm probability for discovery.
