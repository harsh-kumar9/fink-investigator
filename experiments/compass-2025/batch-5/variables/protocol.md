# What changes repeat in the fifth pair of light curves?

Recorded with only the queue identifiers known, before inspecting their direct measurements or catalog identities. Test both ZTF17aaaoxxi and ZTF18abcckqd. Use the gate's chosen red and green DR24 series selected by available catalog epochs and position, flag-free finite magnitudes with positive uncertainty ≤0.2 mag, and one median magnitude and HJD per UTC date. The anchor bounds the primary evidence. Any later DR24 epochs are chronological follow-up.

1. Search for a stable clock over 0.1–1,000 days with a two-harmonic model using only the first two thirds of pre-anchor red nights. Reuse the previously registered ten trial frequencies per resolution element and local least-squares refinement. Test the red shape on later red and green dates, with a green scale and offset learned before the red cutoff. Preserve half/double period and sidereal-day/annual aliases. Use at least 30 dates, 20% squared-error reduction and rank correlation ≥0.5 in each band. If a clock transfers, predict external survey measurements without fitting phase. Compare with prior catalog periods, their aliases, and fixed-red residuals from the primary source. Quantify cross-survey disagreements with neighboring stars and bandpass caveats.
2. Use the frozen 30-day-bin half-year state scan, at least three qualifying bins per side, requiring each bin to contain at least three nights. Test its primary red crossing on green, green-only dates and, if warranted, after removing the fitted clock. If changes exceed 0.5 mag in two bands and survive dropping a bin at 0.3 mag, challenge their sensitivity to missing source detections using a nearby-star calendar, independent forced flux and/or pixel photometry.
3. Search for discrete brightenings with a cut fixed independently of a new object's extreme dates. Define a bright night as at least 1.5 mag brighter than the pre-anchor 80th-percentile magnitude in that filter. Define a return to faint light as within 0.4 mag of that level or fainter. Separate groups of red bright nights only on an observed faint return; mark censoring across gaps longer than 30 days. Require two bright dates no more than 10 days apart and a green bright measurement within ±2 days for a resolved two-band group. Report spans, observed returns and annual coverage. Long smooth periodic bright phases may pass and should be judged against the clock test. Use eruption cadence and duty cycle only after checking sampling and independent data.
4. If the source class in earlier work or these measurements identify a discriminating opportunity, record a separate targeted test before fitting. Never count incidental overlap between selection alerts and ZTF direct photometry as an independent telescope recovery.
