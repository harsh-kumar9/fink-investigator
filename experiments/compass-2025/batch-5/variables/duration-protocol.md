# Do two dwarf novae undergo distinct long and short brightenings?

The source catalog identifies a dwarf nova (MGAB-V960) and a suspected SU UMa dwarf nova (MGAB-V306). We have not yet seen their measured light curves or episode counts. Test whether the available coverage permits separating short and long events in either source. Such durations can motivate a time-series search for superhumps, but duration alone cannot identify the accretion mechanism.

Use the red and green bright and faint thresholds fixed in the initial protocol. Combine bands by UTC date. A date is bright if either band meets its bright criterion with no faint observation in the other band. A date is faint if either band meets its faint criterion with no bright point in the other band. Leave conflicting and intermediate dates unclassified. Separate groups of bright dates when a faint return is observed or when consecutive bright detections are more than five days apart. A long group requires at least four bright dates spanning ≥10 days with no gap >5 days. A short group requires an observed faint date before and after bounding the whole event to ≤7 days. Compare only objects with at least two examples of each class. To reduce contamination by a threshold fluctuation, repeat the analysis with the bright cut changed from 1.5 to 1.25 and 1.75 mag and maximum inter-bright gap reduced from five to four days. Some sparse long groups may be pieces of one eruption. Report the underlying observations, brackets, filters, minimum observed duration, duration upper bound where available and telescope provenance.

Independently query all public ASAS-SN Sky Patrol point sources within 6″, selecting the nearest match within 3″. Use good, finite forced photometry including negative flux, positive flux uncertainties, and dates since 2019 through the anchor. Measure the green-ZTF/ASAS-SN flux rank correlation over all matching dates. For each ZTF long or short event, compare the median independent flux on ZTF bright dates (within one calendar day) with the median on faint dates within one day of each actual bracketing return. Keep only when available, and report camera, flux error, interval and compatible comparison stars when needed. Require at least three independently sampled bright dates on long groups. Distinct degree of crowding and observing depths can conceal true eruptions.
