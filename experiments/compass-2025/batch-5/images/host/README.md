# The pre-event site of ZTF25absffwy in NGC 1037

Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam and DECam show the transient’s position in the southern disk of the barred spiral NGC 1037 (PGC 9973), near the end of its bar. The median position of 55 quality ZTF residuals grouped by 37 observing nights is 39.49389185°, −1.84979310°. Their median nightly radial scatter is 0.072″ and their 90th percentile is 0.211″. The event lies 2.60″ west and 20.47″ south of the Siena Galaxy Atlas nucleus, or 20.63″ in projection. In the figure, the red plus marks the transient, the cyan cross marks the nucleus, and the yellow circle marks the nearest Tractor source in the enlargement. [Field and inset](field.png) · [Measurements](results.json) · [Siena Galaxy Atlas](https://sga.legacysurvey.org/)

The closest three catalog fits are 4.09″, 7.94″ and 8.97″ from the alert. All lie on the structured disk. Treating any of them as an independent host solely by angular separation would miss the much larger galaxy. The central Sérsic model has effective radius 23.62″. DESI DR1 target 39627743108799637 measured the nucleus at z = 0.0283677 ± 0.0000085, with ZWARN = 0. A separate fiber in the field returned z = 0.0283262 with ZWARN = 2. The reliable redshift refers to the galaxy nucleus. [DESI records](sources/redshifts.json) · [Tractor catalog records](sources/tractor.json) · [DESI DR1](https://data.desi.lbl.gov/doc/releases/dr1/)

Hubble observed this same position in 2022. Registering the F555W and F814W images to four Gaia stars gives an aperture excess of 2.58 robust σ in each band. The nearest accepted F555W source is 0.394″ away and carries the extended-source flag. The modest central flux and neighboring sources leave the origin of the ground-based excess open. [Hubble registration, figure and local limits](hst/README.md)

**Pre-existing light.** In both instruments and all three bands, the median signal inside a 1″ radius at the transient exceeded all 54 outer-field apertures. Those controls are at least 30″ east or west of the nucleus and at least 5″ from a modeled optical source. On the disk itself the weak intensity excess has nearby analogues. In HSC g, r and z, one of 12 positions on a local 3″ ring had a higher core-to-surrounding-background contrast than the transient’s site. In DECam the counts were 2, 1 and 8 respectively. [Selection and aperture definitions](protocol.md)

**A local peak close to the transient.** After examining the arm, we subtracted a broad Gaussian-smoothed image from a narrowly smoothed image in each band. Broad Gaussian widths of 0.9″ and 1.5″ both placed a weak local peak 0.23″ from the transient in Subaru g, r and z, and 0.38″ away in DECam g and r. The narrower smoothing width was fixed at 0.22″. The green and red peaks in both instruments remained within 0.5″ when the broad width increased to 2.5″. At that width both z-band peak locations moved outside 0.5″. Image sampling, the sky-coordinate registration and the structured background limit positional precision. The two most similar red and green measurements in different instruments share the same underlying sky. [Local contrast images, centered on the ZTF position](texture.png) · [Numerical sensitivity checks](results.json)

At the 1.5″ background scale, none of 27 comparison positions had a positive peak within 0.5″ in all three HSC bands and both DECam g and r. The comparison grid sampled nearby disk positions of similar underlying HSC r brightness and excluded the centers of catalog sources. We chose the five-band pattern after inspecting the field. This local rank describes the environment and cannot supply a survey-wide probability. The imaging supports a weak patch of pre-existing emission very close to the transient. Arm structure, a stellar association and an unrelated projected source can all contribute at this resolution. Identifying an individual progenitor or H II region requires other evidence.
