# Transfer of the December 2013 two-filter measurement

Recorded before retrieving either Pan-STARRS light curve or viewing its historical epochs. Apply the same test to MGAB-V960 and MGAB-V306, which were selected independently of Pan-STARRS. The previous example brightened by about 2.6 mag in both r and i over six days. Search for an analogous resolved change of at least 2.0 mag in each of two filters taken on both dates, with 1–10 days between those dates and each change exceeding five combined magnitude errors. Also report all possible same-date color pairs and repeat the coverage inventory over the entire available baseline. A longer-baseline change establishes different historical brightness levels without timing an eruption.

Require a single mean source within 1″ or inspect multiple close sources separately. Retain measurements within 1.2″, S/N ≥ 10 and PSF-weighted unmasked fraction ≥ 0.90 as the primary sample. Exclude pipeline defect, failed fit, bad PSF, saturation and blends. Repeat the inventory at S/N ≥ 5 and fraction ≥ 0.85. For qualifying episodes check within-night repeats, aperture fluxes, fixed-position measurements, exact-image comparison stars, and original discovery records. Never turn a missing catalog entry into an upper limit. Report the measured coverage even when either survey test is underpowered.

An exploratory check follows the frozen test. MGAB-V306 had its brightest clean z and y detections on the same date, MJD 56599. Before inspecting fixed-position measurements or field stars, retrieve the full z/y forced-warp series, treating resampled skycells at one time as a single exposure. Report all other usable dates in those bands and the nearest preceding and following measurements, including weak fluxes and limits. Select up to ten neighboring stars within 90″, separated by at least 3″ from every other mean source with at least three detections. Require at least 25 observations, r between 17 and 20, and |rPSF − rKron| < 0.08. Compare checks on the very same skycell warps and earlier or later dates that also observed the target. Count each star once. These comparisons test that the brightening is local. They cannot find its start or end between exposures.
