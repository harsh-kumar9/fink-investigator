# Was excess red light still present after the alerts faded?

AT 2025iac is near the center of a faint extended source. Its forced alert-packet photometry ends June 2, while the DR24 direct-source catalog preferentially detects positive fluctuations and includes red detections into early July. Before measuring archive pixels after July 2, compare forced apertures on original science exposures with the *same sky aperture* before the transient. Restrict this experiment to red images. Use 2023–24 as the pre-event baseline and 2026 as a held-out control for a return to the earlier brightness. Use April 20–May 2, 2025 to check recovery of the known peak. Test June, July, August and September 2025 separately. Preserve failed or missing exposures.

Use science exposures with seeing ≤3.2″, magnitude limit ≥20, accepted infobits (<33554432), and enough isolated same-detector standards for the existing four-pixel radius aperture. Select the field/CCD/quadrant with the most distinct qualifying dates across the four post-peak months that also has at least six pre-event dates and six dates in 2026. Break ties by pre-event date count then numeric detector identity. On each date take the deepest qualifying exposure. Draw up to 24 dates evenly across the pre-event period, 12 across 2026, and eight per 2025 window, independent of measured target flux. Use fixed-position signed flux for every retrieved image, measured with the same aperture and aperture zero points on nearby stars. Compare means in microjanskys, retaining the host in both windows. Report per-night fluxes and uncertainty on the difference from the pre-event mean. Estimate the error on a mean as the larger of empirical scatter divided by the square root of the number of dates and the propagated per-date errors. Require at least four usable nights per window. Report the mean seeing and repeat with pre-event nights whose seeing is within 0.4″ of at least one event-night value and vice versa. Seeing variations across the finite aperture can affect host measurements. Check a second, later baseline against the first, and measure any signal against fixed blank apertures in the same images and a held-out field star when available.

A monthly excess requires at least five standard errors relative to the pre-event baseline and the same direction in the seeing-matched test. Report all four months including failures. Repeated measurements share calibration and sky-position uncertainties. The result constrains light from within the aperture; an isolated point alone cannot establish another outburst.

**Coverage amendment before pixel retrieval.** The two overlapping red detectors have three and five qualifying 2026 nights. Neither meets the six-date detector-selection rule. Retain the four-night minimum for window measurements and select the detector with at least four 2026 nights, field 680, CCD 3, quadrant 3. The stellar catalog has only two qualifying standards within 65″ on either detector. Enlarge the cutout to 225″ and the comparison-star radius to 95″, with the same isolation, magnitude, minimum number of standards and aperture. These choices use exposure metadata and catalog means only. Test fixed blank sky positions in a grid around the source, retaining those separated by at least 12″ from catalog entries. No change to the target position or observing dates.

**Second detector after the primary June result.** Field 680 recovers June excess and gives a lower-significance July mean. Its August window has at most two qualified dates. Apply the same signed aperture, zero-point, quality cuts, sample quotas and contrast to the only other detector with coverage in all four months (field 633, CCD 14, quadrant 1). Its July window has only two eligible dates and its August window has eight. Test all windows and keep the short 2026 comparison. These are additional exposures with distinct calibration stars/detector photometry. Observations from the same date or telescope can share sky conditions and systematics. Compare monthly estimates before considering a joint interpretation.
