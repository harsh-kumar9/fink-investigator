# Can the fourth-batch variables be predicted outside the selection measurements?

Registered with the positions, route assignments and anchor dates known, before inspecting direct-source magnitudes, catalog identities, independent light curves or reviewer conclusions. Apply both tests to ZTF17aabutiy and ZTF18aaadshm. Use the existing DR24 rule (one g and one r series chosen by catalog observation count and position, positive errors ≤0.2 mag and zero flags). Collapse exposures by UTC date using median magnitude and median HJD.

**Brightness prediction.** Reuse the batch-three two-harmonic search in the first two thirds of pre-anchor red dates (0.1–1,000 days). Predict later pre-anchor nights without refitting period or phase. Red coefficients stay fixed. Green amplitude and offset are learned only on dates through the red training cutoff. Report at least 30 later dates, at least a 20% reduction in squared error and rank correlation ≥0.5 per band for a two-band recovery, along with half/double-period, daily, annual and catalog competitors. Use all post-anchor DR24 nights as a second frozen test when available and keep their scores separate. Reuse the registered 180-day state difference and its green coverage cuts from batch three. This is a red-selected interval. A physical change requires corroboration, particularly if an underlying cycle explains the medians.

**Does a source retrace its colors at equal red brightness?** Within each UTC date require green and red median times within 0.12 days. For every color observation, find the nearest earlier and later red nights at least one day and at most 15 days away. Label its local direction only if the two bracketing red magnitudes differ by at least 0.25 mag, with the central red measurement inside that range (allow 0.08 mag tolerance). Require 13 ≤ r ≤ 19.5, g < 20, and do not reuse the center night's magnitude to determine direction. Match a rising night to a fading night from the same calendar year and the same training/held-out subset, at red magnitudes within 0.08 mag. Take the closest red magnitudes first without reusing nights. Use median fading-minus-rising (g−r) per year and subset. Require at least three matched pairs in each of at least three calendar years, median absolute interbranch difference ≥0.12 mag and the same direction in the majority of qualifying early and later years. Test the stricter 0.04-mag red match and 0.06-day interband time gap separately. Report insufficient data, derivative misclassification and different censoring of green as alternatives. A phase-dependent color difference can be physical without establishing its origin.

**Independent observations.** For an apparent clock, evaluate earlier Gaia epoch photometry or external forced flux with unchanged red phase and compare with catalog periods and annual aliases. For a distinct transient or state, recover matched forced flux from another telescope and check whether the relevant transition actually occurs in that window. Choose the archive by sky coverage and catalog counterpart, preserve raw responses and include field controls when measuring a new behavior.
