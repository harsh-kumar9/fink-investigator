# Were eruptions rarer, fainter, or simply missed?

Both sources are cataloged dwarf novae. Neither blind clock transferred to later red and green dates. The first-pass red-selected state windows were centered on February 15, 2021 for ZTF17aabutiy and August 14, 2021 for MGAB-V488. ZTF17aabutiy met the original 30-day-bin criteria in both filters and MGAB-V488 lacked a third earlier green bin. This follow-up was fixed before retrieving secondary detector-series or ASAS-SN fluxes.

1. In both ±180-day windows compare the 20th, 50th and 80th percentiles of nightly magnitudes in each filter. Use the same three quantiles on all nights outside the two windows to fix a bright and faint reference level for each detector and band. Measure the fraction of nights bright enough for the bright reference, and the fraction reaching the faint reference. Give the actual nights, exposures, largest gaps, medians, and the same measures using equal weight per eligible 30-day observing bin. Distinguish changes of the typical measurement or number of detected outbursts from changes in quiescent brightness. Never interpret an exposure missing from an object catalog as a non-detection without exposure evidence.
2. Retrieve all other DR24 object time series within the original 1.5″ cone. Use the original cuts, ±180-day windows and 30-day boundaries on each series, and repeat using only dates absent from the corresponding primary band. For field control use up to three nearby stars within 10–90″ of the target on each primary detector, selected by distance, at least 40 catalog measurements and mean magnitude within 1 mag of the target in the same band. Compare the primary target on exposures shared with at least two controls. Record the target's missing measurements on such field exposures and its magnitude relative to the image's limiting magnitude. Retain catalog flags and comparison-star exclusion counts. If missingness could explain the magnitude change, bound the sampled fraction above the fixed bright threshold by assigning all missing eligible exposures on observed field dates first to faint and then to bright.
3. Query the ASAS-SN Sky Patrol master list within 6″, choosing the nearest row if within 3″. Use finite quality-G forced flux measurements including negative fluxes, positive finite errors, dates after December 2018, and the same unadjusted date windows. Compare medians of 30-day bins with at least three observed dates. Check high-, mid- and low-flux quantiles, counts, camera overlap, and the correlation on same-date ZTF green observations. Report an absent catalog row as missing coverage. Look for published discovery curves and observations in the measured intervals.
4. The previous dwarf nova, MGAB-V243, showed smaller absolute flux differences at 14–20 days than at 6–10 or 24–29 days. Preserve these lag windows and at least ten common anchor dates per year. Apply the same source-independent test to both new objects, in ZTF g and r and any sufficiently sampled independent forced-flux series, without adapting the lags. Yearly agreement in three or more years in each primary filter is evidence for transfer. A negative result is informative about how broadly the earlier cadence generalizes.

**Independent camera and blend checks.** ZTF17aabutiy lost 0.133 mJy in the ASAS-SN window comparison; the archival aperture around MGAB-V488 brightened by 0.203 mJy although ZTF red faded. Both ASAS-SN baselines are bright relative to the ZTF counterparts. Before querying other Sky Patrol time series, select up to three sources 30–60″ from each target whose median good forced flux outside both windows is within a factor of two of the aperture around the target. Select by projected distance among archival sources with data. For each comparison, subtract its median outside-window flux separately by camera, then subtract the median of at least two such source deviations on the identical exposure. Compare the target in the same 30-day bins before and after correction. Also report contrasts by camera, and rank correlation of ASAS-SN flux with ZTF green brightness on common observing dates within each camera. Survey aperture changes, sparse dates and variable neighbors remain possible if the independent aperture fails these checks.
