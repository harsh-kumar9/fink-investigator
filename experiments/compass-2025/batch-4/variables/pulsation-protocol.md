# Does the older RR Lyrae template predict MGAB-V488?

The catalog reviewer found a published Pan-STARRS RR Lyrae candidate fit at 0.4904450424 days with 1.20 mag red and 1.39 mag green peak-to-peak amplitudes. Its low classifier scores and the later VSX dwarf-nova identification make transfer of this particular clock a discriminating test. Register these checks after the broad timing search and before fitting this catalog frequency.

Fit a constant plus two harmonics at the exact published frequency on the original red training dates. Score its unchanged red waveform on later pre-anchor red observations, transfer red shape with a color offset and scale learned on the early green observations, and score post-anchor red/green separately. Also fit each filter independently in the early sample and over a ±0.002-day range around the published period (1001 uniformly spaced frequencies); choose the nearby period by red training fit alone. Report training and subsequent peak-to-peak amplitude separately at the fixed period, along with squared error reductions and rank correlations. A new fit on the later data provides a descriptive amplitude only. Daily sampling can alias the strong unrelated changes of state in this object. Use exposure times in HJD.

Test sensitivity by injecting a sinusoid at the catalog frequency and reported amplitude into the observed ZTF magnitudes, allowing a common phase uniformly sampled at 32 trial values. Fit at the known frequency, train and score as above. Record the number of trials meeting the same 20% prediction gain and 0.5 rank criteria, and the range of recovered amplitudes. A failure to recover even injected variation limits a physical upper bound. Also test whether the sign, amplitude and phase of a putative short modulation repeat in neighboring exposures. On each red or green night with at least two accepted exposures separated by 0.05–0.35 days, take the earliest and latest qualifying pair (widest separation). Their measured difference removes the night's intercept. Fit a sine/cosine difference to early nights, then predict the later pair differences with the unchanged coefficients and score against zero change. Compare fitted amplitudes with the catalog value and repeat injection for the within-night pairs. Use at least ten held-out pairs per filter; record inadequate time coverage. This time-domain test does not require a constant outburst baseline from one night to the next.

**Transfer of the original phased waveform.** The exact-frequency two-harmonic forecast had little predictive power and even full-amplitude sinusoid injection was only partially recoverable. The catalog reviewer subsequently recovered the actual Sesar et al. templates. Before using those shapes on ZTF, register a test that can tolerate unrelated changes of mean brightness. Transfer the published period, phase and the tabulated red and green templates to each clean ZTF exposure using HJD minus 2,400,000. Average observed and expected magnitudes within each date. Measure one common amplitude multiplier for the two filters, allowing an independent intercept for each filter in each 14-day time bin anchored at MJD 58000. Keep bins with at least two dates. Report 7- and 28-day bins as sensitivity checks. This differential test accounts for slower eruption and seasonal changes while allowing filter-specific means. Phase coverage and fast real variability still limit sensitivity.

Compute the amplitude multiplier at the published phase and the greatest positive multiplier across 40 trial phase offsets at the same period. Estimate the upper envelope by resampling contiguous 90-day blocks with replacement 2,000 times, with both filters and all nights in a block kept together; report the 95th percentile. Repeat on later pre-anchor dates alone. Inject the unscaled published pair of waveforms into the actual ZTF magnitudes at every one of the 40 offsets and measure the recovered amplitude at the injected phase. A physical interpretation at this exact period requires an upper envelope below one and recovery within 0.7–1.3 of the injected multiplier for at least 36/40 phases on the full sample. Only detections enter this test. A general constraint on all pulsations or on a different period would require different data.
