# Compare an unclassified transient with spectroscopically classified supernovae

The forced series for AT 2025iac shows a blue early excess and a slower decline in red. The TNS registry has a discovery report and no public type. Before retrieving comparison light curves, select a panel from the ZTF Bright Transient Survey. Use the 2024 calendar year, the BTS coverage-quality flag and observed peak magnitude no fainter than 17.5. The brighter sample leaves room to measure the decline. Keep up to 24 objects per family, choosing the lexicographically smallest SHA-256 digests of ZTF IDs. Use exact catalog types SN Ia; SN II or SN IIP; SN Ib, SN Ic or SN Ib/c; and separately SN IIb and SN IIn. Keep the original type and publicly identifiable SN name. Require a numeric peak time. Report the number selected and the number with usable photometry separately. The panel is balanced by construction and cannot supply posterior probabilities or survey rates.

Use positive difference detections with magnitude error at most 0.20 for the bright controls. Collapse records from the same exposure and bin within one UTC night per band. Use a reference epoch observable in each source, the brightest measured red night within 15 days of the BTS peak (for the target, the April event). Compute the magnitude change from this red peak to the median red magnitude 25–40 observer-frame days later. The target's late forced series spans part of this window. Also compute the change in g−r between days −10 to +2 and +20 to +35, using same-UTC-date pairs. Require at least two distinct nights for every reported window. Magnitudes in each band use the original subtraction flux. Constant foreground reddening and constant distance modulus cancel from the changes. The panel and target can still differ in redshift, cadence, noise and selection.

Compare both quantities at source level and show the individual controls. For a stricter sensitivity check, match control dates to the actual target offsets within two days, one control night per target night per band or color, and reject comparisons with fewer than two distinct nights or fewer than half the available target nights in any window. Give each source one vote. Compare within-class medians and ranges and report coverage failures. Use leave-one-out prediction on the labeled control panel to establish whether these measurements distinguish common classes. Nearest-neighbor comparisons in the two-dimensional space use Euclidean magnitude distance. Report confusion counts, overlapping types and the nearest three distinct controls. This test describes which known light curves resemble the target. A physical subtype requires additional data.

**Registry audit and luminous controls.** The initial panel contains a few transients whose discovery names precede their BTS peak by multiple years. Check the TNS position, type and discovery date of every control against the BTS position and peak. Require a match within 3″, the same family and discovery within 180 days of the compared peak. Preserve each excluded row and the original ranking. Do not refill excluded slots.

The nearest low-redshift example is a Type II, while IA and other classes overlap. The proposed host has a Legacy Surveys photometric redshift near 0.53, of uncertain accuracy at its faint magnitude. Before retrieving an additional comparison panel, query BTS once for classified transients from 2018–2024 with tabulated redshift 0.25–0.80, coverage-quality flag, and peak at or brighter than 20.5. Include spectroscopically classified superluminous Type I and II supernovae, tidal disruption events, normal Ia, Ib/c, II/IIb/IIn using the same family mapping and hash-based quota. Apply the same registry audit, observable-frame measurements, checks on available nights and cadence-matched test. These bright flux-limited samples cannot estimate class incidence. If the target lies in the proposed host, illustrate the luminosity scale using a flat H0=70 km/s/Mpc, Ωm=0.3 cosmology. Quote the observed-band magnitude minus distance modulus, with no extinction or K correction, and the observer-frame timescale divided by 1+z. The host redshift and rest-frame filter are unknown.

The higher-redshift BTS query returned no objects labeled TDE, and one SLSN-II label (AT 2023vto) conflicts with subsequent spectroscopy. Mark that label as disputed. To compare genuine nuclear stellar disruptions, take the complete independent Hammerstein et al. (2023) ZTF-I catalog (30 objects). Retain source-specific H, He and H+He spectral classifications. Use the published dated spectrum closest to optical maximum with absolute phase ≤20 days to identify the event season, using its date minus tabulated phase as an approximate anchor. Use the same ±15-day red-peak search, two observable features and cadence-matched sensitivity test as the other controls. Preserve insufficient and featureless cases. These lower-redshift events are an observed-band check of the nuclear alternative. **Sensitivity to photometric errors and choice of peak date.** After seeing the two measured features and control rankings, perturb the target’s measured nightly signed fluxes 2,000 times by their reported Gaussian errors, keeping the original quality-selected dates. Repeat the red-peak selection and measurement of both features on each draw. Record valid coverage, the distribution of sampled red-peak dates and the closest low-redshift control family. This describes uncertainty in the measurements and phase assignment conditional on the observed dates. It does not include uncertainty in the controls, shared reference systematics, extinction or missed maxima.
