# Independent investigator review of the November outage cases

These initial opinions use only the [frozen two-case packet](results/outage_followup_packet.json). The historical broker lookup failed for both identifiers. The separate successful SIMBAD and VSX queries have radii of 3″ and 5″ respectively. An earlier report under another name remains possible, and the packet contains no independent TNS search. Each case is an alert position pending physical identification. Evidence labels below refer to the packet.

## ZTF18abqclig

**Follow up for recurrent variability.** The November 7 red-band alert lies at (92.6614871°, +20.6345684°) and has a negative residual, consistent with an exposure fainter than its reference. The quality cuts retain 729 detections on 315 dates including 63 dates with positive residuals. This favors recurrent changes in brightness or image subtraction at the position. An ordinary variable blended with a neighbor is plausible. Small registration or image-quality differences in a crowded field could also produce both signs. The Gaia screen disfavors the subset of fast-moving stars it tested. [Alert and history](https://ztf.fink-portal.org/ZTF18abqclig)

Select dates of both signs, then compare the direct-image brightness on those dates and register the science, reference and difference images to local stars. A dipole fixed to a neighbor whose strength varies with seeing would favor subtraction error. A centered change of the right sign in the direct image across several independent nights would favor a varying source. The stellar identity and any catalog alias remain open. The packet evidence includes `alert`, `history`, `motion`, `catalog`, `vsx-cone`, `image`.

## ZTF20aaothpc

**Follow up for an unresolved brightening.** All ten qualifying detections fall on distinct dates with positive residuals. The November 21 red-band alert lies at (130.6710409°, −2.3819350°). The sign pattern supports repeated excess light relative to the reference. The packet does not give the spacing of those ten dates or direct-image fluxes. It cannot distinguish a single episode from recurrent episodes. A faint variable seen only when bright, or a persistent reference-image error, could account for the detections. [Alert and history](https://ztf.fink-portal.org/ZTF20aaothpc)

Reconstruct the dated flux history and inspect direct or forced photometry at the same position on intervening nights without alerts. A source that brightens, fades and returns to baseline with centered residuals would support an episode. A repeatable spatial residual under similar observing conditions would favor a reduction effect. Check transient registries at the coordinates and inspect host offsets before assigning novelty or class. The packet evidence includes `alert`, `history`, `motion`, `catalog`, `vsx-cone`, `image`.
