# Independent first reading of the 2024 packet

I recorded these opinions from the three frozen packet entries before consulting registries, follow-up photometry or another reviewer’s assessment.

* **ZTF18abbtzhm.** On 2 January the source had less green flux than in the reference. Across 259 dates, both filters frequently land on each side of their references. An intrinsically variable star is plausible. A moving source or an unresolved neighbor whose light shifts with seeing could also leave residuals for several years. The strongest test would locate positive and negative measurements on the same reference image, propagate any nearby star’s motion, and check a direct photometric time series for coherent variability.
* **ZTF24abilvns.** A bright positive green residual on 13 October follows six positive green dates since 23 September, five of them also associated with positive red detections. A real, several-week bright episode at a stellar position deserves follow-up. This could be an eruption or a luminous transient projected close to a foreground star. Check the quiescent source and independent measurements before 23 September, compare the position to a precise stellar catalog, and search discovery reports under aliases and coordinates. The first alert does not measure the start of brightening.
* **ZTF17aabunmx.** There are positive green and red detections over many seasons and a minority of negative dates in both filters. A known cataclysmic variable undergoing eruptions is a plausible explanation. The two consecutive 2019 dates could sample the same eruption, and the August 2024 pair could also track one long event. Look for quiescent measurements between apparent peaks and compare direct light curves with published intervals before counting repeated cycles.

## Catalog and discovery checks

**The 2024 September–October brightening was reported at the time.** ZTF24abilvns is consistent with ASASSN-14iy, discovered as a CV candidate on 13 October 2014 at V ≈ 15.3. VSX lists a dwarf nova with a faint-state B magnitude of 20.9. Its 1.07″ catalog offset comes from an older PPMXL position. The only Gaia DR3 source within 5″, 538957063517128832, has G = 20.346 and an epoch-2016.0 position 0.161″ from ZTF. Its proper motion of +12.55, −13.93 mas/year predicts a separation of about 0.005″ at the October 2024 alert. This is a computed angular separation, subject to the uncertainties of both catalogs. VSNET reports of 11 and 12 October 2024 identify ASASSN-14iy by name. They include independent ASAS-SN g = 14.767 on 29 September, 15.107 on 1 October and 15.988 on 8 October, plus ZTF detections. The eruption was documented before the 13 October anchor. The TNS coordinate and alias search and the BTS box return no matches. **The negative-residual source has two published interpretations.** ZTF18abbtzhm is 0.06″ from MGAB-V1042, entered in VSX on 16 October 2019 as a dwarf nova with g = 17.9–20.6, credited to B. Sesar et al. and Gabriel Murawski. At 0.16″, row 165394 of Sesar et al.’s 2017 Pan-STARRS RR Lyrae candidate list gives a template period of 0.5099629760 day and low classifier scores of 0.26 (RRab) and 0.01 (RRc). The PS1 model has fitted g, r, i and z amplitudes of 1.98, 1.52, 1.21 and 1.09 mag and corresponding template IDs 120, 118, 115 and 117. Its phase is the fractional part of (HJD − 2,400,000) / P − 0.39964, with maximum light at phase zero. Its peak magnitudes include a dust correction. The authors used an RRab score above 0.8 for their high-probability illustration. VSX lists no period for this source. The first-pass data leave the survival of the earlier template period in independent observations open. **The recurrent source is a studied SU UMa dwarf nova.** ZTF17aabunmx is 0.22″ from the VSX position of ASASSN-16le. ASAS-SN’s original survey table documents a 2 October 2016 eruption at V ≈ 15.5. The later Gaia transient was reported as AT 2018asi = Gaia18awg, 0.053″ from the ZTF anchor. Sokoliuk et al. studied its optical, UV and X-ray observations. They measured positive superhumps with period about 1.56 hours, estimated a 1.519-hour orbit from the empirical superhump relation, and identified multiple outbursts and superoutbursts. The paper also discusses data back to 2014. The 2024 stream reproduces activity of a well-established system.
