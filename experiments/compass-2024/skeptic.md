# Independent first reading

These assessments use the 2024 packet before looking at catalog identities, images, absolute photometry or the investigator’s interpretation.

**ZTF18abbtzhm.** Both bands crossed their reference flux repeatedly between 2019 and the January 2024 anchor. A periodic star seen at different phases, an irregular variable and subtraction around nearby or moving stars could all produce these signs. Test whether the positive and negative centroids track the same Gaia position after propagation. Inspect early and late residuals for dipoles and compare direct photometry on those dates. An apparent period should predict later observations across filters and seasons.

**ZTF24abilvns.** Six positive dates and five two-color dates over roughly three weeks fit a sustained outburst of a pre-existing star. The star label alone cannot identify the variable component or decide whether the 2024 event was unusual. Use archival images to locate the quiescent object, compare pre-event limits or detections with positive difference flux, and search earlier seasons for similar events. An offset fixed residual on diffuse light would keep an extragalactic transient in play. An isolated low-flux season cannot establish a first-ever outburst.

**ZTF17aabunmx.** A bright green detection on a cataloged cataclysmic variable is compatible with a familiar outburst. The nominated red date pairs could sample plateaus, separate eruptions or simply elevations relative to a faint reference. Compare total flux within and between the nominated intervals, require a return toward a measured baseline between events, and check the source position of both polarities. Prioritize any season with an independently measured duration or amplitude outside earlier well-sampled behavior.
