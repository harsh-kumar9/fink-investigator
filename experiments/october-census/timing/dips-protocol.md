# Can we predict the deep fades at the 5.8-day position?

This is an exploratory extension. Inspection of phase-binned magnitudes in the original ZTF red series, including its later nights, revealed occasional deep fades. That red series informed the question. Test the predictions on the other archived red detector series, using only dates absent from the original quality-cut series. Retain the same nightly quality and tie-breaking rules. A second ZTF field can test a detector artifact and timing on other dates, while blending can affect both fields.

Use the original 304 training dates. Search box-shaped fades within ±1% of the candidate frequency, its two first sidereal-day aliases, and the half-frequency and double-frequency possibilities. For each, sample at intervals no wider than 0.02 cycles across the training baseline. Search durations of 2%, 4%, 6%, 8% and 10% of the central period. Fit the dimming in magnitudes, with a positive depth, using unweighted least squares. Select the family and local fit by training likelihood. Freeze the interval, phase and period. On the other camera series report observed minus out-of-fade median magnitudes inside the predicted interval, all date counts, and the median of the 10th–90th percentile ranges of out-of-fade noise if applicable. Distinguish demonstration on the already examined red series from the additional detector test. Few in-fade nights limit the conclusion.

After the doubled period won, extend its fixed prediction to the previously reduced TESS sector. For every predicted deep fade and intervening half-cycle, measure the deficit in the central aperture relative to the neighboring shoulders. Define shoulders from 1.25 to 3.25 half-widths on either side of the center. Use the line interpolating the median time and flux in the two shoulders. Require at least two six-hour bins each in the event and each shoulder. Report each covered event and the same measurements for all four corner apertures. The TESS frequency comparison was already known when choosing this follow-up. No TESS time or flux enters the predicted centers or widths.

To localize the missing light, take every quality-selected negative green or red Fink detection in the predicted deep window through the October anchor. Use the same DRB and error cuts as the main census. Summarize one position per UTC date and compare to each Gaia DR3 entry in the original 5″ cone, propagated to the date of observation. Report the median and 90th percentile of the separations for each catalog star. These are centroids of the difference-image residual, conditioned on assignment to this alert ID. Subarcsecond components can share them.
