# Can a clock fitted to the early observations predict later brightness?

Freeze this follow-up before running any periodogram. The sample is the six positions and the known RR Lyrae control in the preceding [direct-image test](../photometry-protocol.md). Use the red-band series IDs already selected there. Include every usable exposure of the selected series, regardless of whether Fink issued an alert. Retain the smallest-error exposure per UTC date, breaking ties by exposure ID, with catalog flags zero and magnitude error in (0, 0.2]. Use the archive's heliocentric Julian date for phase.

Fit only the earliest two thirds of the dates, rounding down. Require at least 60 training and 30 later dates. Search periods of 0.1 to 300 days with a floating-mean, unweighted Lomb–Scargle periodogram, ten samples per peak. Refine the highest peak locally on the training set and fit a two-harmonic waveform there. Predict every remaining date with its frequency, phase, amplitude and mean fixed. Compare with predicting the training mean. Report training power, both date counts, held-out squared-error reduction and rank correlation. Flag a timing lead only with at least three training cycles, held-out error reduction ≥0.30 and held-out rank correlation ≥0.60. Require an RR Lyrae control pass before interpreting the six outcomes. Frequencies near the solar or sidereal day and their aliases need an independent cadence. A passing prediction measures phase coherence of the blended light. A physical period can differ by a harmonic.

Separately, for ZTF17aacydyv, test whether the existing W1 and optical changes track each other. Around each accepted W1 visit use the median red magnitude of the chosen direct series within ±10 days, requiring observations on at least two distinct dates. Apply this also to each comparison star's W1 visits against the same target optical series. Report all visit pairs, Spearman correlations for at least five pairs, and leave-one-visit-out ranges. Interpret this small archival comparison descriptively. The original light curves and field-control identities were available in earlier work.
