# An earlier telescope flagged the same position

In 2018 the ATLAS team published a candidate at the position of ZTF18abjtfpx. Their catalog labels **ATO J280.3059+09.7242** `dubious`. It gives coordinates of 280.30599°, +9.72421°, just 0.314″ from the ZTF alert. Its original Fourier-pipeline Lomb–Scargle period was 5.796240 days, within 0.268% of the ZTF training estimate. Its long-period Fourier fit gave 5.793208 days, with the same dominant period in its cyan and orange bands. The fit used 72 cyan and 95 orange measurements. The catalog's separate `lombscar` analysis favored 2.408143 days. These are algorithm outputs from one ATLAS data set, with no period uncertainties in these fields. The 2018 candidate and its published timing establish priority for the positional variability lead. [Published ATLAS row](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/VizieR-5?-source=J/AJ/156/241/table4&recno=2182150) · [Heinze et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02132)

ATLAS recorded a Pan-STARRS neighbor at 3.7″, with the closest neighbor of equal or greater brightness at 27.6″. Its proximity indicator was 1, which means the survey's own test favored intrinsic variability. Its difference-image indicator was 0. The 15″ VizieR cone returned 16 Gaia DR3 rows, one ATLAS candidate, zero ASAS-SN 2021 variables and zero Gaia DR2 large-amplitude entries. The nearest Gaia star has DR3 source ID 4479632717757053440 at J2016.0 and a predicted 0.254″ separation at the Fink alert epoch. The 2.85″ and 3.67″ Gaia neighbors at J2016.0 matter for assigning the signal. The three nearby Gaia sources each carry `VarFlag=NOT_AVAILABLE`. An earlier, spatially resolved Pan-STARRS check adds evidence for the closest source. At 0.352″ from the Fink position, the 5.81178-day fit accounts for 42.8% of the scatter after fitting separate mean magnitudes for the three included filters, compared with 5.6% for the competing 2.408143-day ATLAS estimate. At the neighbor 3.68″ away the corresponding fractions are 2.6% and 2.7%. The brighter source contributed 20 filter-night medians and this neighbor contributed 16. Phase and amplitude were fitted on these sparse measurements. This comparison favors the brighter source over that specific neighbor. The 2.905″ neighbor has only six usable filter-nights and a fourth, two-detection Pan-STARRS entry at 3.089″ has zero after quality cuts. Their possible contributions remain open.
