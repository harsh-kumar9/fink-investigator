# Can we tell which star changed brightness?

An independent reviewer (*gpt-6-luna*, 2026-11-25) examined the archived histories, catalog cones and three image triplets. It favored recurrent variability near a faint Gaia star and left the source identity and physical class open. Its interpretation was recorded before the direct-image test results were supplied to it. The data were shared with the main analysis, so the two readings do not constitute independent observations. [Image triplets](images/gallery.md) · [Catalog extraction](../../sources/extended/ZTF17aacydyv.json)

We checked the review's numerical claims. Gaia DR3 3429463387935153536 has a mean G magnitude of 18.4604. The October anchor is 0.244″ from its J2016.0 position and 0.221″ after linear proper-motion propagation. The archived history includes 356 detections, 340 through the selected packet. Of those, 256 pass the quality cuts on 215 UTC dates, including 169 dates with a positive residual. Alerts in 2019, 2023 and 2026 establish recurrence. Fink's alert magnitudes measure the flux difference from an older reference. They cannot by themselves give the magnitude of the whole source. [Fink history](https://ztf.fink-portal.org/ZTF17aacydyv) · [ZTF field definitions](https://zwickytransientfacility.github.io/ztf-avro-alert/schema.html)

The predeclared follow-up tested the corresponding red-band brightness measured on the original images. On 130 matched dates the direct measurement and the signed difference flux had Spearman rank correlation 0.995. The direct median was 0.805 magnitudes brighter on the 124 positive dates than on the six negative dates. The data support changing brightness at this position. The small number of negative dates, shared exposures and unresolved neighbors limit the inference. Centroid measurements at different brightnesses, sharper images and independent color or spectral measurements would distinguish the candidate star from a blend and constrain its class.
